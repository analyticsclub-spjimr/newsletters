AI × MARKETING

Half your ad money is wasted. AI now knows which half.

By Srihari.

Picture this, you wake up one morning, stretch and reach for your phone and open the first news article you see. It is about the same old AI in the workplace topic and it tells you for the umpteenth time on how AI is taking over the workplace AGAIN, which is causing layoffs AGAIN. “Not this again” you groan and scroll up and see an ad for a toothpaste. “Hmmm, suspicious” you think. Especially at this time of the day and after you have just woken up. But this is the least of what AI can do in digital marketing nowadays.

No matter how varied the opinion is on AI nowadays, with some people swearing by it, that it makes their lives easier, while others seem sick of it and repeatedly tell you that it is a massive word prediction software, (myself being in the latter), there is no doubt about the advancements it has achieved in everyday life and industry, one such space being digital marketing.

“Half the money I spend on advertising is wasted; the trouble is I don’t know which half.”

Attributed to John Wanamaker, the 19th-century retail titan

If John were alive today and walking through the quad after a brutal Marketing Management case prep, an ensemble gradient-boosted model would tap him on the shoulder, hand him a dashboard, and say: “Actually, John, it was precisely 48.7% of ad set B on Tuesday at 2:14 AM. Also, your creative fatigued three days ago.”

Welcome to modern digital marketing, where gut feelings and apparent natural creativity are trumped by neural networks, and the intersection with AI looks less like a neat Venn diagram and more like a high-speed collision inside a Snowflake warehouse.

Enter machine learning. The goal is no longer just tracking where a user went yesterday; it is predicting where their intent will land tomorrow. Marketing analytics has shifted from descriptive post-mortems (“Why did nobody buy our farm fresh milk?”) to prescriptive dynamic interventions:

01

Multi-touch attribution without the hallucinations

Traditional last-touch attribution gave 100% of the credit to the final search ad, ignoring the podcast mention, the YouTube pre-roll, and the three weeks of subconscious brand conditioning. Bayesian attribution models and Shapley-value formulations now weigh the true incremental lift of every channel touchpoint before marketing budgets are incinerated.

02

Hyper-personalization at scale

We’ve graduated from “Hello {First_Name}” email marketing. GenAI and reinforcement learning loops now tailor value propositions on the fly. A price-sensitive student studying near Andheri gets ad copy highlighting bundle discounts and immediate delivery, while an impulse buyer gets scarcity framing, rendered in real-time by dynamic creative optimization (DCO) engines.

03

Synthetic personas and pre-testing

Why blow millions on a nationwide focus group when you can prompt fine-tuned agentic clusters that simulate your demographic cohorts? Marketers are running synthetic testing rounds on copy, sentiment, and visual assets before spending their first media dollar.

The “black box” dilemma (or, when the AI goes rogue)

Of course, letting neural networks run your brand voice is a bit like handing a flamethrower to an over-enthusiastic MBA intern who is over caffeinated and simultaneously very hungry to prove themselves. A deadly and disastrous combo if you ask me personally.

Algorithms optimize strictly for the loss function you hand them. If you tell an engine to maximize clicks without guardrails, it will gladly write clickbait so sensational that your premium B2B SaaS platform begins to look like a tabloid exposé on the latest Bollywood party. Furthermore, algorithmic bias and data drift mean that what drove conversions during Diwali might crash your ROAS completely by February.

Marketing effectiveness requires equal parts Python script and behavioral economics. The data tells you what happened down to the millisecond; human intuition and brand strategy tell you why a customer cared enough to click. This is where you as a manager come in.

What it means for the next generation of managers

If you think marketing in the AI era means sitting back while prompt generators run your agency on autopilot, you’re looking at the wrong half of the dashboard. The brands winning today aren’t the ones spamming automated content; they’re the ones building closed-loop data pipelines, understanding customer lifetime value (LTV) anomalies, and treating every campaign like a live statistical hypothesis.

The algorithm has solved the targeting puzzle. The next challenge is making sure the message is actually worth reading.
